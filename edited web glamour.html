Model Interview

"Glamour isn’t just in the fabric – it’s in the fearlessness to wear who you are." — Nyla K.

🔸 Cultural Threads, Modern Muse
Nyla K. is not just a model — she’s a statement in motion.

Wearing bold prints inspired by her heritage and pairing them with modern silhouettes, Nyla represents a generation of models embracing their cultural identity unapologetically. Her look isn’t just fashion-forward; it’s soul-rooted.

“For me, glamour isn’t about sparkle — it’s about confidence,” she says. “It’s about knowing who you are, where you come from, and wearing that with pride.”

Born in a small town but raised in the heartbeat of urban fashion, Nyla blends Afrocentric patterns with street-style elegance. Her braided hair, layered jewelry, and effortless pose speak louder than any runway strut. She’s the image of a future where fashion celebrates authenticity, not uniformity.

As the modeling world continues to evolve, faces like Nyla’s are redefining beauty — not as something to chase, but something to own.

The Backstory

Under the broad umbrella of The Duck Magazine, a proud brand of Ratcom Studios, Model Glamour was born in the vibrant heart of Mityana, Uganda.

In this small, picturesque town, visionary Martin Ssembuule saw beyond the surface. Surrounded by a rich tapestry of Ugandan tradition, vibrant colors, and enduring community pride, he recognized that the authentic style and untold stories of Mityana were being overlooked by mainstream fashion.

Inspired by the raw, unfiltered beauty of his hometown, Martin set out to create a platform where fashion was more than trends—it was a narrative of resilience, heritage, and transformation. Model Glamour was conceived as a celebration of self-expression and authenticity, an invitation to honor personal stories and cultural identity.

Today, as Model Glamour graces the digital pages of The Duck Magazine, its journey from the humble streets of Mityana to global runways stands as a testament to Martin’s original vision: real glamour is born from genuine self-acceptance and pride in one’s roots.
